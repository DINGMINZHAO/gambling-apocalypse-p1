\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

This study investigates Smart Money in Polymarket by examining forecasting skill, historical trading information, and changes in betting size. The results provide several insights into how predictive information is reflected in wallet behavior. First, historical forecasting performance exhibits statistically significant persistence, with more pronounced signals among experienced wallets. The Empirical Bayes analysis further demonstrates that accounting for estimation uncertainty improves the identification of historically successful wallets with stronger future performance. These findings indicate that historical forecasting records contain useful information, and that appropriate statistical estimation plays an important role in extracting persistent skill.

Second, wallet trading behavior contributes to forecasting accuracy beyond market probabilities in several predictive experiments. Historical trading information also demonstrates potential incremental value in controlled comparisons. The results suggest that wallet behavior can provide a complementary source of information for probability forecasting, particularly when combined with market expectations. Third, the analysis of betting conviction reveals an interesting relationship between behavioral changes and market uncertainty. Larger bets are frequently placed on outcomes already favored by the market, while their additional forecasting advantage is most pronounced when market probabilities are close to 50\%. This pattern suggests that the informational value of trading behavior may vary considerably across different market conditions.

Taken together, the findings suggest that Smart Money identification can benefit from incorporating both historical forecasting performance and dynamic trading behavior. Wallet experience, previous trading activity, changes in betting size, and market uncertainty provide complementary perspectives on the information contained in prediction market transactions.

Several aspects of the analysis warrant further investigation. Historical market probabilities may lag transaction times by up to 12 hours, while the availability of market outcomes is partly approximated. In addition, the research sample is determined by the availability of historical prices and resolved outcomes, and some comparisons depend on wallets remaining active in subsequent periods. Dependencies across related markets and events may also influence statistical inference. The betting conviction results are exploratory, as multiple behavioral comparisons were examined and the final evaluation period was inspected during the research process. Additional market periods and more precise transaction time probabilities would help assess the stability of the observed relationships.

Finally, the analysis focuses on predictive information rather than trading profitability. Translating these findings into practical trading strategies requires further consideration of transaction costs, market liquidity, execution prices, and trading opportunities. These issues provide a natural extension toward the investigation of Smart Money trading intelligence.
